YOU ARE NOW ENTERING CONTROLLED AIRSPACE

Listen to real air traffic control communications from the world's busiest airports: real pilots, real controllers, streamed live by the volunteer scanner network at LiveATC.net.

How this works: Every station below has a one-tap Quick Listen that streams that airport's tower frequency right here on the page. Nothing loads until you press play, and starting a new station stops whatever was already playing. “Listen via LiveATC” opens that airport's full live stream directly on LiveATC.net in a new tab, where you can pick the exact frequency (tower, ground, approach) currently online, the most stable option if a quick-listen feed is down. These are volunteer-run feeds, so they occasionally go offline for maintenance or interference; that's normal.

What You Might Hear

ATC phraseology is deliberately terse and standardized worldwide. Here's a quick key to a few phrases that come up constantly.

“Cleared for takeoff” / “Cleared to land” Explicit permission to depart or land on a specific runway. Nothing moves without it.
“Roger” vs. “Wilco” “Roger” just means the message was received and understood, not agreement. “Wilco” means “will comply.”
“Squawk [code]” An instruction to set a specific transponder code. “Squawk 7700” is the universal code for a general emergency.
“Go around” An aborted landing: the aircraft climbs back out to try the approach again, for reasons ranging from routine spacing to a real problem.
“Heavy” A callsign suffix for aircraft over 300,000 lbs, warning other pilots to expect wake turbulence behind it.
“Say again” A request to repeat the last transmission, used constantly, and never a sign anything is wrong.
