Legends Of Flight

Aviation History

From a twelve-second flight on a windy North Carolina beach to supersonic passenger travel, these are the moments that turned aviation from a dream into the safest way to cross the planet.

December 17, 1903 · Kitty Hawk, NC

The First Powered Flight

— December 1903

Orville Wright flew 120 feet in 12 seconds aboard the Wright Flyer, shorter than the wingspan of most airliners today, and the single moment that started everything.

Read full report →
May 20–21, 1927 · New York to Paris

Lindbergh Crosses the Atlantic Alone

— May 1927

Charles Lindbergh flew the Spirit of St. Louis solo across the Atlantic in 33.5 hours, turning aviation overnight from a curiosity into a global obsession.

Read full report →
July 2, 1937 · Near Howland Island, Pacific Ocean

Amelia Earhart Disappears

— July 1937

Attempting to circle the globe by air near the equator, Earhart and navigator Fred Noonan vanished near Howland Island. No confirmed wreckage has ever been recovered, and eighty-nine years later, a Purdue-led expedition is still actively searching for the wreck.

October 14, 1947 · Mojave Desert, CA

The Sound Barrier Falls

— October 1947

Chuck Yeager piloted the Bell X-1 past Mach 1, proving that supersonic flight, long believed to be impossible or fatal, was survivable and controllable.

Read full report →
August 1, 1955 · Groom Lake, Nevada

The U-2 Spy Plane Takes Flight: By Accident

— August 1955

Built in secret by Lockheed's Skunk Works in roughly 88 days, the U-2 made its first flight unintentionally, lifting off during a taxi test at a secret Nevada test site that would become known as Area 51. It flew so high Soviet defenses couldn't touch it. The CIA has since confirmed it caused more than half of America's UFO reports in the years that followed.

1958 · The Dawn of the Jet Age

Commercial Jets Change Everything

— 1958

The introduction of jet airliners cut transatlantic travel time nearly in half, made flying accessible to the middle class, and reshaped how the entire world traveled, worked and vacationed.

Read full report →
May 1, 1960 · Sverdlovsk, USSR

Gary Powers Is Shot Down Over The Soviet Union

— May 1960

CIA pilot Francis Gary Powers' U-2 was struck by a Soviet missile deep inside Soviet airspace. He survived, was captured and tried for espionage, and was exchanged twenty-one months later on Berlin's Glienicke Bridge, a Cold War incident that publicly exposed the secret program and humiliated President Eisenhower.

March 27, 1977 · Tenerife, Canary Islands

Aviation's Deadliest Day

— March 1977

Two Boeing 747s collided on a fog-covered runway, killing 583 people in the deadliest accident in aviation history. The tragedy directly led to standardized aviation English phraseology and modern Crew Resource Management, changes credited with preventing countless accidents since.

1976–2003 · Twenty-Seven Years of Supersonic Travel

Concorde: Flying Faster Than Sound, Commercially

— 1976

Concorde carried passengers across the Atlantic in under three and a half hours at twice the speed of sound, a feat of commercial aviation engineering that, decades later, still hasn't been repeated.

Read full report →
December 26–28, 1980 · RAF Woodbridge, Suffolk, England

Rendlesham Forest: The US Air Force's Most Documented UAP Case

— December 1980

US Air Force security police at a NATO base in rural England reported a craft in the forest outside their fence over three nights. Deputy base commander Lt Col Charles Halt personally investigated and recorded eighteen minutes of the encounter on a pocket tape recorder, since declassified. It remains the most thoroughly documented military UAP encounter on record.

Read our full case file →
August 12, 1985 · Osutaka Ridge, Gunma Prefecture, Japan

Japan Airlines 123: Aviation's Deadliest Single-Aircraft Accident

— August 1985

A Boeing 747's rear pressure bulkhead, repaired incorrectly seven years earlier, failed twelve minutes after takeoff from Tokyo, tearing away the tail and severing all four hydraulic systems. The crew fought for 32 minutes using only engine thrust. 520 of 524 people aboard died; it remains the deadliest accident in history involving a single aircraft, and directly shaped how aircraft repairs are inspected worldwide.

Read our full case file →
December 21, 1988 · Lockerbie, Dumfries and Galloway, Scotland

Pan Am 103: The Deadliest Terrorist Attack In British History

— December 1988

A bomb hidden inside a radio cassette player exploded aboard Pan Am Flight 103 at 31,000 feet, killing all 259 people aboard, including 35 Syracuse University students flying home for Christmas, and 11 more residents when wreckage fell on the town of Lockerbie. The bombing led directly to mandatory baggage reconciliation, now standard practice at every airport in the world.
