July 2, 1937 · Near Howland Island Intensity 6/10

Amelia Earhart Disappears

— July 1937

Attempting to circle the globe by air near the equator, Earhart and navigator Fred Noonan vanished near Howland Island. No confirmed wreckage has ever been recovered, and eighty-nine years later, a Purdue-led expedition is still actively searching for the wreck.

August 1, 1955 · Groom Lake, Nevada Intensity 5/10

The U-2 Spy Plane Takes Flight: By Accident

— August 1955

Built in secret by Lockheed's Skunk Works in roughly 88 days, the U-2 made its first flight unintentionally, lifting off during a taxi test at a secret Nevada test site that would become known as Area 51. The CIA has since confirmed it caused more than half of America's UFO reports.

March 27, 1977 · Tenerife, Canary Islands Intensity 10/10

Aviation's Deadliest Day

— March 1977

Two Boeing 747s collided on a fog-covered runway, killing 583 people in the deadliest accident in aviation history. The tragedy directly led to standardized aviation English phraseology and modern Crew Resource Management.

December 26–28, 1980 · RAF Woodbridge, England Intensity 7/10

Rendlesham Forest: The US Air Force's Most Documented UAP Case

— December 1980

US Air Force security police at a NATO base in rural England reported a craft in the forest outside their fence over three nights. Deputy base commander Lt Col Charles Halt personally investigated and recorded eighteen minutes on a pocket tape recorder, since declassified.

August 12, 1985 · Osutaka Ridge, Japan Intensity 10/10

Japan Airlines 123: Aviation's Deadliest Single-Aircraft Accident

— August 1985

A Boeing 747's rear pressure bulkhead, repaired incorrectly seven years earlier, failed twelve minutes after takeoff from Tokyo. The crew fought for 32 minutes using only engine thrust. 520 of 524 people aboard died.

December 21, 1988 · Lockerbie, Scotland Intensity 9/10

Pan Am 103: The Deadliest Terrorist Attack In British History

— December 1988

A bomb hidden inside a radio cassette player exploded aboard Pan Am Flight 103 at 31,000 feet, killing all 259 people aboard, including 35 Syracuse University students flying home for Christmas, and 11 more residents on the ground.
