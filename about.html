About Flight Crew Files

Independent aviation journalism from inside the industry.

What We Cover

The world of aviation, all in one place.

Flight Crew Files is an independent aviation platform dedicated to exploring every corner of the aviation world. From airports to airspace, commercial airlines to military aviation, private jets to helicopters, aircraft technology to aviation history, and the people who keep the industry moving. We bring together aviation news, careers, training, safety, travel, aircraft, operations, insights, and inspiring stories for aviation professionals, travelers, and enthusiasts worldwide. Whether you're on the ground, in the cockpit, in the cabin, behind the scenes, or simply fascinated by flight, welcome aboard.

Who We Are

An insider perspective no press release can replicate.

Flight Crew Files was founded by Jhemica, a former cabin crew member who served as a Beauty Ambassador for one of the world's most prestigious Middle Eastern airlines. With years of firsthand experience in commercial aviation, she brings an insider perspective that no textbook or press release can replicate.

That background shapes what this site is for. Aviation writing tends to split into two camps: technical material written for people already inside the industry, and dramatised retellings written by people who have never worked a cabin. We are trying to do the third thing — accurate, sourced, readable accounts of what actually happened, written by someone who knows what the job feels like.

What We Publish

Full Case Files, Built From Primary Documents

Long-form investigations of real accidents and incidents, each one built from the official report rather than from other people's summaries of it.

How We Verify

Official Record First, Story Second

Every factual claim is cross-checked against at least two independent sources, anchored wherever possible on the government investigation itself.

Where We Say We Don't Know

Uncertainty Stays Uncertain

Where an official report says a cause could not be determined, we say so too, rather than resolving it for the reader.

Our Mission

Told completely, and told accurately.

We believe aviation stories deserve to be told completely and accurately. Every case file on this site is researched from primary sources — official accident reports, declassified government documents, and verified eyewitness accounts. We never speculate without evidence. We never sensationalize without fact.

Editorial Standards

The rules we hold ourselves to

Sourcing

Official Investigation Reports First

All case files are sourced from official accident investigation reports where one exists. Where none exists in English — as with Ethiopian Airlines Flight 961 — we say so on the page and name the secondary sources we relied on instead.

Access

No Paywalled Sources

Everything we cite is publicly accessible. If you want to check our work, you can — without a subscription, an institutional login or a request form. Every source link on every case file is live and free to open.

Accuracy

Corrections Published On The Page

We update and correct errors promptly, and we mark the correction where the error was, not in a log nobody reads. Several case files already carry notes flagging where the popular version of a story diverges from the official record. See our corrections policy.

Independence

No Affiliate Influence

No affiliate relationship, advertiser or commercial arrangement influences our editorial content. We are not affiliated with any airline, manufacturer, regulator or investigative body, and we do not accept payment for coverage.

Images

Properly Attributed And Licensed

All images are properly attributed and licensed. Photographer, source and licence appear in the caption of every photograph on this site, and we use freely licensed material — overwhelmingly Wikimedia Commons and government works — rather than material we cannot document.

Sources

Contributors Stay Anonymous

Reader and crew submissions are edited and stripped of identifying detail before publication. People still working in the industry talk more openly when their name is not attached, and protecting that is a sourcing practice, not a way of avoiding accountability for what we publish.

Our Sources

Where the facts come from

These are the authorities our case files are built on. Where a report exists, we link directly to it.

United States National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB)

Primary source for US accident investigations, including our Alaska 261, TWA 800, United 232, Air Florida 90 and Miracle on the Hudson case files.

United Kingdom Air Accidents Investigation Branch (AAIB)

The UK investigating authority. Report 4/1990 is the backbone of our Kegworth case file.

Australia Australian Transport Safety Bureau (ATSB)

Australian investigations and the search records underpinning the MH370 case file.

France Bureau d'Enquêtes et d'Analyses (BEA)

The French investigating authority, and the source of record for Air France 447.

Canada Transportation Safety Board of Canada (TSB)

Report A98H0003 on Swissair 111 is one of the most thorough accident investigations ever conducted, and our case file follows it closely.

International International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO)

Annex 13 sets the framework every investigation on this site was conducted under.

United States, Regulatory Federal Aviation Administration (FAA)

Airworthiness directives, the Lessons Learned library and released communication transcripts, including the FAA transcript behind our Miracle on the Hudson radio quotations.

Declassified Records CIA Reading Room And Government Archives

Declassified CIA and other government documents, used in the U-2, Rendlesham Forest and TWA 800 files.

Images Wikimedia Commons

Effectively every photograph on this site, each one credited to its photographer with its licence stated in the caption.

One More Thing

This is journalism about aviation, not a substitute for official investigation.

Flight Crew Files is an independent enthusiast publication. We are not an airline, regulator, investigative body, or affiliated with any government agency. Incident details are drawn from official reports and public reporting; UAP case files reflect accounts as described by the witnesses and agencies involved, and we present those accounts without claiming to resolve them.
