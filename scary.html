The Night Lockerbie Burned

— December 1988

A bomb hidden inside a radio cassette player exploded in the forward cargo hold of Pan Am Flight 103, tearing the nose from the Boeing 747 at 31,000 feet over Scotland. All 259 people aboard died, including 35 Syracuse University students flying home for Christmas.

Featured Case File Intensity 10/10

The Flight That Lasted 32 Minutes Too Long

— August 1985

Twelve minutes after Japan Airlines Flight 123 left Tokyo, a rear pressure bulkhead repaired incorrectly seven years earlier gave way. The tail tore off. All four hydraulic systems failed at once. For 32 minutes, the crew fought a 747 that no longer had a way to steer. 520 people died.

Featured Case File Intensity 7/10

The Night The US Air Force Met Something They Couldn't Explain

— December 1980

Over three nights after Christmas 1980, US Air Force security police stationed at RAF Woodbridge, a NATO base in Suffolk, reported a craft in the forest outside their fence. The deputy base commander recorded eighteen minutes of the encounter on a pocket tape recorder, since declassified.

Featured Case File Intensity 9/10

The Plane That Fell From The Sky

— June 2009

Over the mid-Atlantic at night, ice crystals blocked all three of an Airbus A330's speed sensors at once. The autopilot disconnected exactly as designed, and two first officers spent the next four minutes fighting an airplane neither of them realized was stalled. It fell for three minutes and thirty seconds.

Featured Case File Intensity 10/10

The Day Two Jumbos Collided

— March 1977

A bomb threat diverted dozens of flights to a tiny, fog-bound airport in the Canary Islands. Hours later, two Boeing 747s ended up on the same runway at the same time, and 583 people died without either aircraft ever leaving the ground.

MH370: The Plane That Vanished

— March 2014

A Boeing 777 carrying 239 people vanishes from radar over the South China Sea with no distress call, then flies on, alone and unresponsive, for nearly seven more hours into the Southern Indian Ocean. Most of the aircraft has never been found.
